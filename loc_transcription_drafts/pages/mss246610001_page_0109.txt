We then discussed a possible reciprocity
Treaty. I said Can must give us Rail
in some must'd goods.
Laurier said he could not now give
us any better terms than to England - he
would have done this 3 or 4 years ago
but that was now passed. He suggested
lower duties on barley hay and lumber
and said coal also old but included.
Stronghuny suggested RR cars as a
good subj. for lower duties - they were
poorly made in Canada & sold for
only a little less than Amer. steel +
duty. Davis suggested wooden shoes,
we told them - returning to seal question
the Be. Col. fishermen would be glad to
sell out - they said they did not
understand this. We asked them to
look at us.
Laurier said Canada might be driven
to retaliate on U.S. & might put an
upshot duty on wood pulp.
We discussed subj. as to whether fed.
Govt old Reful. Fisheries or front
lanes.

1897
11:10
1 Foster thinks I should go to Montreal to see
Van Horn or Stronghuny and have them
impress upon Laurier the necessity of
solving the seal question.
2 Went to N.Y. Took 7.30 train for Montreal.
3 Lord. In Montreal. Satisfactory talk w.
Stronghuny
4 Thursday. In Boston. Dined in evening
with Mr. & Mrs. Prescott Hall and Miss
Pruyn of Albany. Went to Boston Athenæum
to see play called "Never Again" - found it
carn & vulgar - left theatre and went to
Park to see "Girl from Paris". Found this was
about as bad - left again & we all went
to University Club on my invitation - to
supper. Took 12 P.M. train for N.Y.
5 Took 8 am train from N.Y. to Washington.
arrived 1.45. Called on Col. & Mrs. Rice.
Treaty was to have been signed today
