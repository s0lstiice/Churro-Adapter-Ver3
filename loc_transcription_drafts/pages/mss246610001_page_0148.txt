I told him that I never again could
give any assurances as to my future
action as that must never be judged
by circumstances over which I might have no
control. I had no objection however to
saying that I had no present intention
of going into politics again or running
for Gov'; that I had this present situation
however which I had no reason to
withdraw from him - of laying to be
unbroke elected delegate at large to the coming
National Convention, and that I intended
to use my very best efforts to secure
this.
He then again said that he would
use every influence in his power to
bring this about and that his friends
of their own motion favored this.
While he did not specifically mention
Easton's name in this connection or
that of the Demo. machine, yet he
left no doubt possible among
gentlemen that in Easton's behalf and
that of the machine he voluntarily
offended me the position of delegate
at large so far as I, and said machine
had power to control him.

He then spoke very bitterly of Williams and
said he did not go as a delegate to the
Convention. I advised him to go very
slow as to this - in my judgment it
would be well to try as far as possible
to harmonize the Party - and that without
harmony Easton did do nothing next
year. He was very bitter and said
Williams opposed Almy so that a
Western man did he nominate for President
and he Williams for Vice President.
I told him frankly that I should
have no part in any factional fights
within the Party and especially, deprecate
any attempt to cast the so called
Begun element; that if I were elected
delegate at large it would be only as a
representative of the whole Party; that
if he and those whom he represented
gave me their support for Dilly at
large it must be entirely without any
understanding or agreement with me
as to my future action; that I felt now
that all wings of the Party were friendly
to me and that I appreciated such
friendly feeling and intended to do
nothing to forfeit it.
