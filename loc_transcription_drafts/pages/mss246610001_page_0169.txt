May Meeting of delegation at Hotel Erskine.
I was elected unanimously the Hon.
member of the Com e in Resolutions.
June 6 arrived home after 2 weeks at court of
Robert Bruce in the Admiralties. Called
on Mr. Albany. He told me that just
after I left for N.Y. about May 22 he
wrote Collins that he declined to
allow his name to be presented at the
Nat. Convention - said also he had written
Cleveland to same effect, it was very
much surprised as he had informed
me he would do nothing in the
matter without hearing what it had
to say. After some talk he said that
Collins had not answered his letter
and he vainly intimated that he
thought he could say that being so
instructed by the Convention he must
present it. He said he did not
want the nomination and that no one
realized better than he what a weak
candidate he would be, it finally
left him.
The next day I telephoned Albany
to
Collins & he luncheoned with me. He showed
me a copy of Albany's letter & said he
had called on him yesterday afternoon.
He said he had understood Col. to change
our sentence of his letter & send him
a new draft of the same date. He
further said he had had a long
letter with him and that in his
judgment we should go on working
just as hard as ever & he would leave
the responsibility. He undoubtedly had
worked some misunderstanding with
Albany by which he felt able to continue
working for him. He said no one knew
of the letter except one of his clerks
and Jim Donovan but that neither of
them would reveal it. I told him dear
Henry, almost sure of it & I would continue
him. It suggested why it might not be
advisable to tell Easter but he said
No, that this was a private matter
between Mr. Col. & himself.
