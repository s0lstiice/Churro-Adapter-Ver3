Sept 22 Went to N.Y. w. Burnett and thence to New
Orleans Sept 29. Before leaving Robert
Burnett told me over telephone that I
need not worry that Mr. Always name
will be presented to the Convention -- not
perhaps in such a way as to force him
either to affirm it or deny it but in
such general terms as to leave no doubt
of its meaning.
Sunday. John Cunlly told me that
Moore of N. Adams told him that it
was well understood that Easton
was going to try to buy the State
election. He is one of his strong supporters.
The Board. bankers Synd. Am. announced
in cordially instructed news items
that the machine & Easton had decided
that Olney should not be endorsed.
Sept 25 Guston asked me to nominate him --
I accepted having been already
advised by Olney, Coughlin & McConnell
to do this. Mr Griffin of Irving, Rep.
over telephone from Denver said that was
the only thing for me to do.
Sept 29
Tuesday At Eastons request lunched with
301
him at Cyclo Club. He told me he had
decided after full consideration not to
have Olney endorsed, although he was
personally friendly to him. He said he
found William & the Bryan men will
profit if this were attempted. Almost
in same breath he told me that the
shots of Cinic. (Aug 4) had told him
that Bryan told C. he would accept
Olney. He told me this was a done
job and that he confidently
expected to be elected.
I gave him my opinion positively that
endorsing C. would help him but he
could not be turned from his
purpos.
Am satisfied if elected I will be entitled
to be a candid himself.
I tried to get Burnett on telephone to
ask him to explain why he had
changed his views but he was away
& could not return till after the
Convention.
G. told me he had persuaded Mr
Moore (?) of B. Her. that it was not
exped. to endorse Olney -- he alone
was the editor. writer who favored
endorsement.
Buttles Munroe.
