In New York; saw G. T. Passee; met Thomas
S. E. Morris, Editor of Indianapolis Sentinel
a deleg. at large to Convention; he favored
our own Bill.
Then, by appointment, called on Mr.
Cleveland at Victoria Hotel; had a half hour
call & saw the baby; found them both
very charming.

(35.B.39)
June 11. Sunday and I thought it would be ad-
viseable to have a lunch & invite 8 or 10 of
the next Cleveland men on the delegation
to Natl Convention & others. We called on
Henry R. Pinckney and he at once said he
would give it. Took place at Union
Club at 1 P.M. Mr. Pinckney was ill and
could not attend but Mayor Matthews
presided in his place. The sentiment was
cultivistic for Cleveland: Mr. Patrick
Maguire made an eloquent speech.

Coleman - myself - Josiah Quincy - Robert M.
