70
Divid at Chamberlaynes w. Mr. Shinnery, Editor of
N.Y. Times, Mr. Dummell, Wash. Corresp. of N.Y. Times,
Antietam; Meddows and Maxwell & Lurie Carden
U.S. Rev. Marine
"18 Went to Boston to try and arrange as to
some app't for J. Aubrey Byrne; Henry
R. Reed was opposing him. Stopped at
New York and had conference with the
Tariff Court. Corn. took night train
for Boston. Took 8.15 train for Gray
Reed to bring some out. Rev. Coll's
papers to Presd. Cleveland. Had long
interview with him; he signed all
the appoints as Carlisle requested.
We talked over financial situation;
he was very indignant w. silver man
ipule. Senator; so if they wanted a
fight he would give them one -- ask to
hold Tilgh. Sen. Silas who had written him
urging holding back appoints that his
idea was a good one and that he had
better see the cabinet ministers and support
it. He did not approve of the declaration
attached to Vorhees Beee reported to Senate.
He advised me not to be a candidate
for Governor at present.
71
From Gray, Hobbs, I rode to Albany & reached
on Wed. at Albany; saw Henry Reed there -- he
agreed to waive all objections to Byrnes
appointment except for Secy. Agent. I then
left for Boston. Reed let me & manager of
2d Natl. R.R. to hold Bangor express for
min's if I went late -- also told him to
give steam at his with excuse to leave me
to Shools. On reaching Portsmouth, the
Stn master met me & drove me to
Navy Yard landing where the steam
Lantern "Dixon" belonging to Hotel Wentworth
was awaiting me. I went on
Board but in a few minutes we ran
aground in the River. Went back to
Portsmouth & spent night. Went to
Shoals next morning. Delayed at
Shoals over Monday by terrible storm.
Aug 31 Went to Boston to dine with Holmes
Editor of Herald to which criticizing Sec.
Carlisle: had very pleasant interview
& he agreed to order his edit. writers to
write him more fairly & to give him
credit for the great work he has
done in the Silver fight.
Schots
